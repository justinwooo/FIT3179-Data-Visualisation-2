URBAN GREEN SPACE · MELBOURNE

How Green Is Your Neighbourhood?

Some of Melbourne’s greenest-looking municipalities are not necessarily the easiest places to reach a local park. Explore the difference between parkland area, the kinds of spaces available, and the land covered by walking catchments.

01 / The green landscape

One city, very different kinds of green

A bushland reserve, a sports oval and a neighbourhood garden all appear green on a map, but they serve different purposes. This story separates parks and sportsfields from the broader network of natural land and recreation corridors.

1. Inner Melbourne stands out for everyday parkland

The City of Melbourne has approximately 13.8% of its land classified as accessible parks, gardens or sportsfields; Port Phillip follows at about 12.2%. Large outer municipalities are not necessarily ahead once parkland is compared with total municipal land.

2. The same green footprint can mean very different spaces

Natural and semi-natural land plays a much larger role in Yarra Ranges, while Melbourne has a more prominent share of parks and gardens. The contrast matters: natural reserves and everyday recreation spaces should not be treated as interchangeable.

3. Parkland comes in very unequal parcel sizes

The spread of parcel sizes is enormous: a few very large natural or conservation parcels sit alongside many much smaller sites. A simple count of green-space records would obscure these differences.

02 / Green for whom?

More parkland does not always mean more per person

Total hectares reward municipalities with more land. Accounting for resident population can reorder the rankings considerably—revealing places whose apparently substantial parkland must serve many people.

The outer municipalities Yarra Ranges and Cardinia appear near the top after population adjustment, but this measure still says nothing about how close parks are to homes. It should be read alongside the walking-access maps.

03 / A park nearby?

A green municipality is not necessarily a walkable one

Yarra and Whitehorse have roughly three-quarters of their municipal land inside the mapped 400m catchments, compared with just 12% in Yarra Ranges. The difference is striking, but much of the outer area is sparsely populated.

How to read these figures: The 400m measure is the percentage of land covered by historical walkable-catchment polygons—not the percentage of people living near a park. Rural land can strongly affect an LGA’s result.

7. Walking catchments are concentrated in the urban core

Yarra (75.8%) and Whitehorse (74.2%) have some of the highest mapped land coverage. Cardinia (15.8%) and Yarra Ranges (12.3%) are among the lowest, reflecting a very different urban landscape.

Choropleth · 400m catchment land coverage, not population access

8. Does more parkland guarantee better walking coverage?

Municipalities with similar parkland shares can have different mapped walking coverage. That separation is central to the story: the size of a green-space network and its geographic reach are not the same measurement.

9. Large parkland totals can hide uneven geographic reach

Some outer municipalities have substantial total hectares but relatively low catchment coverage. Size and colour deliberately encode different measures, making that contrast visible on the same map.

04 / Everyday recreation

A local park is more than a patch of green

The type of open space—and what people find within it—changes how it can be used. Land-area statistics tell us little about facilities, suitable activities or everyday convenience.

10. What kinds of public open space dominate?

The mix differs sharply across Melbourne. Where natural and semi-natural land dominates, total green-space area can overstate the share represented by conventional local parks and sportsgrounds.

100% stacked bars · category areas may overlap across types

11. Redefining “green space” reshuffles the picture

Yarra Ranges changes dramatically when natural and semi-natural space is counted, whereas inner-city differences are generally narrower. The choice of definition can therefore alter which municipality appears best supplied.
