How Green Is Your Neighbourhood?

02 / The green landscape

One city, very different kinds of green

This section separates and provides insights into the distribution of different types of green spaces: [1] Natural open spaces, [2] Parks and Gardens, [3] Recreation Corridors, [4] Sportsfields and Recreation Areas.

1. Everyday Recreational Parkland (% of LGA Area)

The City of Melbourne has approximately 13.8% of its land classified as accessible parks, gardens or sportsfields; Port Phillip follows at about 12.2%. Large outer municipalities are not necessarily ahead once parkland is compared with total municipal land.

2. Green-Space Categories by LGA (% of Land Area)

Natural and semi-natural land plays a much larger role in Yarra Ranges, while City of Melbourne has a more prominent share of parks and gardens.

3. Distribution of Public Open-space Area Size

Open-space area varies substantially across different types of open spaces. Sportsfield and Recreation Areas are typically the largest, reflecting the space needed for organised sport. Parks, Gardens, and Recreation Corridors are typically the smallest, indicating that smaller neighbourhood spaces are common across Melbourne.

03 / Green for whom?

How is everyday parkland shared across municipalities?

This section explores the size of individual green spaces, parkland available per resident, and the influence of population density on green-space provision.

4. Cumulative Distribution of Open-Space Area Sizes

Around 85% of recreation corridors are smaller than 0.1 ha, compared with a much smaller share of parks, gardens and sportsfields. Sportsfields generally occupy larger areas, while parks and gardens fall somewhere in between, reflecting their different recreational purposes.

5. Everyday Parkland per 1,000 Residents by LGA

The outer municipalities Yarra Ranges and Cardinia appear near the top after population adjustment, but this measure still says nothing about how close parks are to homes. It should be read alongside the walking-access maps.

Everyday parks and sportsfields hectares per 1,000 residents - top 15 LGAs

6. Parkland per Resident by Population Density

Lower-density municipalities generally have more recreational parkland per resident. For example, Yarra Ranges has 7.46 ha per 1,000 residents, compared with just 1.11 ha in high-density Glen Eira. However, more parkland per person does not necessarily mean better access, as parks may be spread across larger areas and located further from residents.

04 / A park nearby?

A green municipality is not necessarily a walkable one

What is a 400m walking catchment? An area reachable within approximately 400 metres of walking along pedestrian-accessible routes from a public open space.

Yarra and Whitehorse have roughly three-quarters of their municipal land inside the mapped 400m catchments, compared with just 12% in Yarra Ranges. The difference is striking, but much of the outer area is sparsely populated.

7. 400m Green-Space Catchment Coverage by LGA

Mapped 400 m catchments cover around three-quarters of municipal land in Yarra (75.8%) and Whitehorse (74.2%), but only 15.8% in Cardinia and 12.3% in Yarra Ranges. Fringe LGAs contain extensive less-developed land, so these figures describe geographic coverage, not how many residents live near a park.

Choropleth · 400m catchment land coverage

8. Recreational Parkland vs. Walking Catchment Coverage

Whitehorse reaches 74.2% catchment coverage with everyday parkland accounting for just 4.5% of its land. Yarra Ranges records 12.3% catchment coverage and 0.5% everyday parkland. The contrast suggests that where parks are located matters alongside how much land they occupy.

13. Green spaces in the City of Melbourne

A municipal percentage can conceal neighbourhood-level gaps. 13.8% of the City of Melbourne's municipal area is parkland, yet only 55.5% of residents are within a 400-metre walking distance of parkland.

05 / Everyday recreation

A local park is more than a patch of green

Having more green space does not always mean having more parks and recreational facilities. Yarra Ranges, for example, has extensive natural open space but relatively little everyday recreational parkland. This section compares these different types of green spaces and associated facilities such as playgrounds.

10. Composition of Public Green-Space Categories

The mix differs sharply across Melbourne. Most of the public green-space in Yarra Ranges consists of Natural and semi-natural Open Space (98.1%). In comparison, most of the public green-space in Glen Eira consists of Sportsfields (73.5%). City of Melbourne has the highest percentage of Parks & Gardens in its public green-space area (55.8%).

100% stacked bars

11. Broad Green Space vs. Everyday Recreational Parkland

Broad green space includes natural reserves and recreation corridors, while everyday parkland focuses on parks, gardens and sportsfields. Yarra Ranges has around 30.8% broad green-space coverage but just 0.5% everyday parkland. Similarly, Hobsons Bay falls from approximately 21.5% to 6.4%. These differences highlight how total green-space coverage can overstate the land available for everyday recreation.
